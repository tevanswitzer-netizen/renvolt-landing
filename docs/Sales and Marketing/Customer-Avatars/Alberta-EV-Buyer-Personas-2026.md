## Persona 1 — "The Practical Albertan" *(Primary)*
> *Not an eco-warrior. A financially literate homeowner who did the math.*

### Demographics

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 48–65 |
| **Gender** | 60% male / 40% female |
| **Household Income** | $95,000–$175,000+ |
| **Education** | Post-secondary degree or trades certification |
| **Housing** | Owns a single-family home with attached garage |
| **Location** | Edmonton metro, St. Albert, Sherwood Park, Spruce Grove, Leduc |
| **Household Type** | Multi-vehicle; 1–2 ICE trucks/SUVs + 1 EV for daily commuting |
| **Employment** | Oil & gas adjacent (engineering, project management, trades leadership), healthcare, government, finance |

### Psychographics

- **Deeply value-conscious** — Will not pay a premium without a clear, quantified financial return. Environmental motivation is secondary to economic motivation
- **Identity-cautious around EVs** — Does not want to be seen as "anti-oil sands" or politically progressive; frames EV ownership as "smart spending"
- **Truck-and-SUV identity** — The vehicle is functional (towing, camping, winter roads). Any EV considered must credibly handle these use cases
- **Peer-validated decision maker** — Trusted word-of-mouth from a neighbour, colleague, or brother-in-law is the single highest-converting influence
- **Privacy-oriented** — Skeptical of data collection, app-required charging, subscription models that "change" a product they paid for

### Motivations (What Makes Them Buy)

1. Fuel cost savings — quantified ("saves me $200/month")
2. Home charging convenience — "plug in overnight like a phone"
3. Low maintenance ("no oil changes, ever")
4. Financial peer influence ("my neighbour got one and loves it")
5. Federal EVAP rebate reducing upfront cost barrier

### Frustrations (What Slows or Stops Them)

- No provincial EV rebate + $200/yr registration tax = perceived financial penalty
- Winter range anxiety — real concern, not irrational
- Charging on highway corridors (Edmonton–Jasper, Edmonton–Fort Mac) is genuinely sparse
- Broken or occupied chargers on critical routes = trust destruction
- EV marketing feels preachy or politically coded; triggers identity resistance

### What They Say (Direct Quotes From Research)

> *"I like the idea of saving on gas, but I'm not going to be stuck in -30 with a dead battery."*

> *"I still need my truck for the farm. But if there was a reliable station on the way to Jasper, I'd consider an EV for the highway runs."*

> *"The government is taxing me $200 a year to drive an EV? Why would I bother?"*

### Messaging That Wins With This Persona

| ❌ Avoid | ✅ Use |
|---|---|
| "Reduce your carbon footprint" | "Cut your fuel bill by $2,000 a year" |
| "Clean energy future" | "Charges overnight for less than $3" |
| "Green vehicle" | "Smarter spending. Same roads." |
| "Sustainable transportation" | "Never pay for gas on your commute again" |
| Anything that sounds like federal government messaging | Local, peer-voiced testimonials |

### Where They Spend Time (Marketing Channels)

- **Radio:** CHED, 630 CHED, IHeart Edmonton
- **Digital:** Facebook (50+), YouTube (how-to/reviews), Google Search
- **Print/OOH:** Edmonton Journal, highway billboard corridors
- **Community:** AMA member (high trust in AMA endorsement), Tim Hortons, Costco regulars
- **Peer networks:** Neighbourhood Facebook Groups, workplace coffee rooms, golf courses

### Renvolt Touchpoint Moments

| Moment | How Renvolt Shows Up |
|---|---|
| Route planning for Edmonton–Calgary drive | Renvolt listed on PlugShare and ABRP with real uptime data |
| Peer recommends EV | Renvolt station co-branded with AMA = instant credibility |
| First public charge attempt | Premium, well-lit plaza; clean restrooms; coffee nearby = confirms decision |
| Charging fails elsewhere | Renvolt Reliability Promise: "We cover your roadside if we fail you" |

## Persona 2 — "The Oilfield Professional Pragmatist" *(High-Value Secondary)*
> *Works in energy sector, earns $120K–$200K+, is ideologically skeptical but financially open to an EV as a second vehicle.*

### Demographics

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 35–55 |
| **Income** | $120,000–$250,000+ |
| **Housing** | Homeowner, likely acreage or large suburban lot |
| **Vehicle** | F-150 or RAM 1500 as primary; open to adding an EV as commuter |
| **Location** | Fort McMurray commuters, Edmonton metro, Parkland County, Sturgeon County |

### Psychographics

- Views EVs as ideologically "other" but is not irrational — responds to pure financial logic
- Has a garage, has the income, has the infrastructure — just needs the right framing
- Would never admit an environmental motivation; will freely admit a financial one
- Highly skeptical of government programs; trusts peers and financial advisors

### The Winning Pitch

> *"Your F-150 is your truck. This is your fuel savings machine."*

> *"It costs $4 to drive to downtown Edmonton and back. Every day. You'd break even in 3.5 years."*

### Renvolt Role

This persona charges almost exclusively at home for daily commuting. Renvolt earns trust with this persona by being **on the Fort McMurray highway corridor** — the one route where this driver genuinely needs a fast, reliable charger.

## Persona 3 — "The Tech-Forward Urban Millennial" *(Growth Segment)*
> *Urban professional, values-driven, blocked by apartment charging access.*

### Demographics

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 28–42 |
| **Income** | $65,000–$110,000 |
| **Housing** | Rents condo/apartment in Edmonton core, Oliver, Glenora, Windermere |
| **Vehicle** | May not currently own a car; or drives a compact ICE |

### Psychographics

- Wants an EV badly; delayed by inability to charge at home
- Brand-aware: Tesla is aspirational, Hyundai Ioniq 6 is practical
- Heavy social media user; responds to peer content and influencer reviews
- Will be the fastest-adopting segment once MUD charging infrastructure scales

### Renvolt Role

This persona uses **public charging as a primary charging source** — Renvolt's most frequent user type in urban settings. Target with:
- Urban Level 2 locations near apartment districts
- Renvolt Pass subscription (price certainty matters to this income bracket)
- Instagram/TikTok presence showing Renvolt as part of urban EV lifestyle

## Persona 4 — "The Fleet & Sustainability Officer" *(B2B)*
> *Corporate or government fleet manager under ESG mandate pressure.*

### Demographics

| Attribute | Profile |
|---|---|
| **Age** | 40–58 |
| **Title** | Fleet Manager, Sustainability Director, VP Operations |
| **Organization Types** | Oil & gas operators, AHS, Government of Alberta, universities, logistics |
| **Decision Criteria** | TCO, reliability, ESG reporting compliance, driver satisfaction |
| **Buying Cycle** | 6–18 months; requires RFP process at larger organizations |

### Pain Points

- Needs to justify EV fleet transition to a CFO with hard numbers
- Requires depot charging infrastructure, not just public access
- Drivers resistant to EVs (especially in trades/field roles)
- Charging reliability failure = operational downtime = direct cost

### Renvolt Value Proposition (B2B)

*"We design, install, and operate your depot charging infrastructure under a managed-services agreement — so your CFO sees a single line item, your drivers see working chargers, and your ESG report shows verifiable emissions reductions."*
