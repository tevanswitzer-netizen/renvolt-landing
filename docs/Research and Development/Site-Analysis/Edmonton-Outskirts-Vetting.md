## 1. Executive Summary & Strategic Framework

As co-founders, our core expansion thesis at Renvolt is the **"EV Desert & Gateway Strategy"**. While national networks (Electrify Canada, Petro-Canada) and Tesla are locking horns inside dense urban cores (like central Edmonton and Calgary), their business models require massive, unbuffered grid connections. This leaves the key highway outskirts—where inter-city travelers experience peak range anxiety—wide open.

By capturing the outlying gateways of Edmonton, Renvolt can construct a **"Suburban Ring of Moats"**. Any electric vehicle entering or exiting the capital region in any of the four cardinal directions will pass a premium, battery-backed Renvolt hub.

## 2. South Outskirts: Leduc / Nisku / EIA (Calgary Corridor)

### 2.1 Geographic & Consumer Profile
* **Target Site:** Premium Outlet Collection Edmonton International Airport (EIA) / Nisku Commercial Zone.
* **Corridor:** Queen Elizabeth II Highway (Highway 2) — Canada’s highest-density inter-city corridor outside of Ontario/Quebec, carrying **35,000 to 45,000+ vehicles daily**.
* **Primary Demographics:** Inter-city commuters, airport/rental vehicle fleets, and high-dwell retail shoppers.

### 2.2 Competitive Landscape & PlugShare Audit
* **Premium Outlet Collection (EIA):** Currently hosts four **Level 2 Flo chargers** charging at a glacial 7.2 kW, costing $2.00/hour, with **critically low PlugShare ratings due to hardware failures and frayed cords**.
* **Leduc Recreation Centre & Downtown Leduc:** Slow city-owned 50 kW Flo units. Require a 10-minute highway detour, charging $10/hour.
* **Surepoint Group (Nisku):** 50 kW SWTCH charger in an industrial lot. High rate of offline/broken status and poor lighting.
* **Tesla Superchargers:** A "Coming Soon" marker exists at 1000 Airport Rd, but it is not live. Active stations are deep in Edmonton (Southgate / Currents).

### 2.3 Grid Feasibility & The Renvolt Advantage
* **Utility Provider:** Nisku, Leduc, and the EIA area fall under **FortisAlberta** (distribution) and **AltaLink** (transmission), *not* EPCOR.
* **The Renvolt BESS Solution:** FortisAlberta charges steep monthly demand charges for peak draws (a standard 600kW station triggers **$8,000 to $12,000/month in fixed charges**). By pairing our chargers with a **100 kW / 100 kWh battery storage system (BESS)**, we can draw a steady, low-amperage 50 kW trickle from the grid and discharge at 150kW+ to the vehicles, bypassing Fortis's peak penalties and long connection delays.

## 3. West Outskirts: Spruce Grove / Stony Plain / Acheson (Jasper Corridor)

### 3.1 Geographic & Consumer Profile
* **Target Site:** Spruce Grove Highway Commercial Plazas (Walmart/Canadian Tire corridor) OR Acheson Industrial Area.
* **Corridor:** Highway 16 (Yellowhead Highway) West — the primary Trans-Canada route connecting Edmonton to Spruce Grove, Stony Plain, Edson, Hinton, and Jasper/BC.
* **Primary Demographics:** Mountain tourists and weekend recreational travelers, suburban commuters, and commercial fleets in Acheson.

### 3.2 Competitive Landscape & PlugShare Audit
* **Spruce Grove & Stony Plain:** Total **black hole** for public fast charging. Both municipalities only host Level 2 SWTCH stations (16 units total, taking 4-8 hours to charge) at sports parks, public works yards, and city halls.
* **Acheson & Winterburn:** Zero public fast chargers of any kind along the highway approach or inside the industrial zones.

### 3.3 Strategic Grid Scenarios: FortisAlberta vs. EPCOR
* **Scenario A: Acheson or Spruce Grove Retail Strips (FortisAlberta Zone):**Punishing demand charges, but massive retail space. An exceptional location to leverage our BESS-backed system on a standard retail electrical feed, avoiding years of utility grid-upgrade delays.
* **Scenario B: Winterburn Road / 215 St NW (EPCOR Zone):** Lower demand charges inside Edmonton city limits, but real estate is heavily cramped and highly expensive, with limited room for NFPA 855 fire separations for battery containers.

## 4. East Outskirts: Sherwood Park / Hwy 16 East (Refinery & Lloydminster Corridor)

### 4.1 Geographic & Consumer Profile
* **Target Site:** Broadmoor Boulevard Commercial Corridor OR Wye Road retail plaza.
* **Corridor:** Highway 16 (Yellowhead Highway East) connecting Edmonton to Lloydminster, North Battleford, and Saskatoon. 
* **Primary Demographics:**
  1. **Industrial Fleet Traffic:** Heavy transport, construction, and refinery support vehicles serving Strathcona County's "Refinery Row" and industrial heartland.
  2. **Inter-provincial Travelers:** Tourists and commercial travelers heading east toward Saskatchewan.
  3. **Suburban Shoppers:** Captive commuter audience in Strathcona County (~100,000 population) utilizing local retail plazas.

### 4.2 Competitive Landscape & PlugShare Audit
* **Sherwood Ford (Broadmoor Blvd):** Hosts a public **320kW FLO Ultra DC Fast Charger**.
  * *⚠️ The Dealership Hurdle:* While fast and 24/7, dealership-based chargers suffer from significant consumer drawbacks. They are frequently blocked by dealer vehicle inventory, lack nearby 24/7 food or beverage amenities, and create an exclusionary, awkward experience for drivers of competing vehicle brands (e.g., Hyundai, Kia, Tesla).
* **Canadian Tire & Strathcona County Facilities:** Only host slow, legacy Level 2 FLO or municipal charging.
* **Vegreville Supercharger:** A 120 km drive east on Highway 16, leaving a massive gap on the immediate outskirts of Sherwood Park.

### 4.3 Strategic Verdict
* **Score:** **82/100** (Site Scoring Matrix)
* **Verdict: 🟢 GO (High-Capacity Fleet & Commuter Node)**
* **Co-founder Take:** Sherwood Park represents an outstanding opportunity for an **independent, retail-anchored fast-charging hub**. By avoiding dealership territory and partnering with a premium shopping center (e.g., off Broadmoor or Wye Road), we can offer a 24/7 accessible hub with quick-service dining (Tim Hortons, Starbucks) and clean restrooms. This will capture the inter-provincial traffic and local commuters who refuse to charge at a car dealership lot.

## 5. North Outskirts: Morinville / Hwy 2 North (Slave Lake Loop Corridor)

### 5.1 Geographic & Consumer Profile
* **Target Site:** Highway 2 / Morinville commercial corridor (e.g., travel stop / gas plaza).
* **Corridor:** Highway 2 North — the primary northern artery connecting Edmonton to Morinville, Athabasca, and feeding directly into the **Slave Lake (Phase 2A)** and **Lac La Biche (Phase 2B)** corridors of our Northern Loop.
* **Primary Demographics:**
  1. **Northern Corridor Travelers:** Recreational and commercial drivers traveling between Edmonton and northern industrial centers (Slave Lake, Athabasca, Fort McMurray).
  2. **Suburban Commuters:** High daily commuter traffic from Morinville and Sturgeon County entering Edmonton.

### 5.2 Competitive Landscape & PlugShare Audit
* **St. Albert (St. Albert Centre):** A **12-stall Tesla Supercharger** (with universal compatibility) has been planned at St. Albert Centre. Beyond this, St. Albert only offers slow, city-owned Level 2 chargers.
* **Morinville (Sturgeon County Centre):** Hosts **zero** DC Fast Chargers. Only Level 2 FLO chargers are available at the municipal center.
* **Northern Highway 2 Corridor:** Virtually no fast chargers are active for the 130 km stretch between Edmonton and Westlock/Athabasca.

### 5.3 Strategic Grid Scenarios & Northern Loop Integration
* **Grid Mechanics:** Morinville is a **FortisAlberta zone**. Pulling massive instant power is cost-prohibitive due to grid congestion and demand charges. The Renvolt BESS-buffered solution allows us to install a high-speed hub on a low-amperage commercial feed.
* **The Slave Lake Connection:** A Morinville hub is the **ideal Northern Gateway launchpad**. In our [Alberta-5-Node-Corridor-Loop-Analysis.md](file:///c:/dev/Web/company-landing/docs/Research%20and%20Development/Site-Analysis/Alberta-5-Node-Corridor-Loop-Analysis.md), Leg 5 (Lac La Biche → Edmonton) is a long 215 km stretch that causes range anxiety, particularly in winter. By placing a node in Morinville, we bridge the gap, tightening our northern loops and providing a bulletproof transition point for all northern travelers.

### 5.4 Strategic Verdict
* **Score:** **89/100** (Site Scoring Matrix)
* **Verdict: 🟢 STRONG GO (Gateway loop Anchor)**
* **Co-founder Take:** While St. Albert will likely be saturated by the St. Albert Centre Tesla Superchargers, **Morinville** is a wide-open gateway. Placing a 4-stall battery-backed hub in Morinville captures the travelers *after* they leave the city limits, right before they hit the long northern transit lanes. It acts as the perfect northern transition point, eliminating range anxiety and supporting our Slave Lake loop.

## 8. Strategic Deep-Dive: South Airport Mall (EIA) vs. South Edmonton Common (Urban Edge)

To optimize our capital allocation on the southern entrance, we evaluated moving our proposed outskirt site slightly north into the city limits at **South Edmonton Common**—one of Canada's largest open-air retail developments. Below is the strategic and operational comparison:

### 8.1 Grid and Utility Profile (FortisAlberta vs. EPCOR)
*   **South Airport Mall (Nisku/EIA): Served by FortisAlberta.** Fortis operates with extremely high peak capacity demand charges, which makes standard fast-charging installations financially toxic. However, this is the **ideal operational window for our BESS-buffered chargers**, letting us draw a slow 50kW trickle to charge our batteries while delivering 150kW+ to travelers, avoiding Fortis's peak fees and grid upgrade timelines.
*   **South Edmonton Common: Served by EPCOR.** As Edmonton's municipal utility, EPCOR has lower, more predictable commercial tariffs and demand fees. However, South Edmonton Common's local distribution grid is **already heavily saturated** due to massive big-box anchors (IKEA, Walmart, Rec Room). Installing a high-power charger here without a battery buffer would likely trigger massive upstream substation connection fees and a protracted EPCOR approval cycle (12+ months).

### 8.2 Traffic Dynamics and User Friction
*   **South Airport Mall (Nisku/EIA): True Corridor Capture.** Located directly on Highway 2 (QE2), this site has seamless, high-speed on/off ramp access. Inter-city travelers and airport rental fleets can pull in, charge, and return to the highway in under 30 minutes with zero internal retail traffic gridlock.
*   **South Edmonton Common: Local Retail Congestion.** While highly visible at the Gateway/Henday interchange, South Edmonton Common suffers from **severe internal traffic gridlock**. Navigating the sprawling parking lots and multi-lane internal ring roads can add 10–15 minutes of pure friction just to reach a charger. Corridor travelers heading to Calgary actively avoid detouring into South Common due to this congestion, meaning our target customer base would shift from high-margin highway travelers to lower-margin local shoppers.

### 8.3 Vandalism and Physical Security (The Copper Theft Factor)
A critical, real-world differentiator emerged during our PlugShare audit of South Edmonton Common:

*   **South Edmonton Common: Extreme Security Risk.** The open-air, sprawling parking lots of South Common are a **frequent target for cable cutting and copper theft**. Multiple charging stations in the area (managed by SWTCH at 98 St NW, ChargePoint at MEC, and legacy Tesla Destination plugs) are currently **offline, deactivated, or completely vandalized with cut cables**. Deploying a high-value DCFC site here would require substantial extra CapEx in security infrastructure (fenced-off cages, 24/7 active CCTV, heavy steel-conduit cable armor, and high-intensity lighting).
*   **South Airport Mall (Nisku/EIA): High-Security Haven.** Siting our hub on airport-adjacent commercial land (like the Premium Outlet Collection) provides an **extremely secure environment**. Airport property is constantly patrolled by EIA security/police, heavily lit, and under continuous high-definition surveillance. Vandalism, loitering, and cable theft are virtually non-existent in this perimeter, radically reducing our ongoing OpEx and maintenance overhead.

### 8.4 Head-to-Head South Corridor Recommendation
While South Edmonton Common offers massive local shopping draw, the **South Airport Mall (Nisku/EIA)** remains the **undisputed winner for Renvolt**:
1.  It captures the high-margin, long-distance traveler *before* they dissolve into Edmonton’s urban grid.
2.  It acts as a high-security sanctuary, shielding our capital assets from the copper-theft epidemic plaguing South Edmonton Common.
3.  It maximizes the financial arbitrage of our BESS system under FortisAlberta's punishing demand-fee structure.
